Educational Measurement and Tests

Measurement, aptitude and achievement, tools, the measurement cycle, types of test and their functions.

Measurement

Measurement is the assignment of numbers to describe human behaviour. It is the quantitative description of a given affective or psychomotor domain of human behaviour.

Thorndike (1981): measurement is the assignment of numbers to attributes, objects, events or persons according to rules.

In teaching and learning, measurement is the process of quantifying the change in a learner's behaviour after teaching has been completed. The data help the teacher, the learner, the parent or guidance counsellor make decisions about the learner's progress.

Plain English
Measurement turns what a learner can do into numbers, so people can decide what to do next.

Educational measurement and measures of ability

Educational measurement focuses on measures of ability: what a person or group is capable of doing. Measures of ability divide into two:

MeasureMeaning
AptitudeWhat an individual can do in future with training
AchievementWhat an individual has acquired or attained after a course of instruction
Plain English
Aptitude looks forward (what you could do). Achievement looks back (what you have gained).

Item Analysis

Difficulty, discrimination and distractor indices, with a calculator.

Item analysis is the process of analysing examiners' or students' responses to individual test items to assess the quality of the test as a whole.

Why do item analysis?

  1. Guide the teacher in selecting final items and rejecting weak ones.
  2. Ensure the distractors are appropriate.
  3. Arrange items in a sequential order.
  4. Identify defective items.
  5. Identify areas where students have or have not mastered.

Process of item analysis

  1. Arrange the testees' scores in order of magnitude.
  2. Divide the distribution into three parts (upper, middle, lower) using 27%, 27% or 33⅓%.
  3. Select the upper and lower classes of scores or scripts.
  4. Compute the item analysis indices: difficulty, discrimination and distractor index.

The three indices

IndexFormula (as written in the notes)Reading it
Difficulty (P)P = (U + L) / N0 means very difficult. 0.3 to 0.7 is moderate. 0.7 upward is simple (easy).
Discrimination (D)D = (U − L) / (½N)Ranges 0 to 1. Below 0.30 is poor. 0.30 and above is good.
Distractor (T)T = (L − U) / NPositive means a good distractor. Zero or negative means weak.
SymbolMeaning
UNumber of testees in the upper group who got the item right (for T: who chose the distractor)
LNumber of testees in the lower group who got the item right (for T: who chose the distractor)
NNumber of testees in both upper and lower groups together (for D and T the notes use ½N, the number in either one group)
Plain English
Difficulty: how many got it right. Discrimination: did the strong students beat the weak ones? Distractor: did the weak students fall for the wrong options more than the strong ones?

Explain the difficulty index, with its formula and interpretation.

Item difficulty measures how difficult an item is.

P = (U + L) / N

U and L are the numbers in the upper and lower groups who got the item right; N is the total in both groups. Interpretation: near 0 is very difficult; 0.3 to 0.7 is moderate; 0.7 upward is simple.

📍 See Difficulty Index
Likely exam question

Explain the discrimination index, with its formula and interpretation.

The discriminating power of an item is the extent to which it distinguishes the better learners from the weaker ones.

D = (U − L) / (½N)

It ranges from 0 to 1. Below 0.30 is poor (not discriminating well). 0.30 and above is good.

📍 See Discrimination Index
Likely exam question

What is a distractor and how is a good distractor identified? Give the formula.

A distractor is a wrong option that diverts the testee from the right answer. A good distractor is chosen more by the lower group than the upper group.

T = (L − U) / N

Good distractors have a positive index. A distractor index of 0 or negative is weak.

Validity and Reliability of an Instrument

Types of validity, forms of reliability, the Pearson formula and a calculator.

Validity

Validity is the extent to which a test or instrument measures what it is intended to measure.

TypeMeaning
Content validityWhether the instrument is a representative sample of the content (wording unclear in the notes)
Construct validityWhether the test accurately measures a theoretical construct such as intelligence, anxiety or personality
Criterion-related validityTwo kinds: predictive (the test predicts future performance) and concurrent (the test score is compared with another already established test)

Factors affecting the validity of a test:

  1. Length of the test
  2. Language content
  3. Ambiguity of the test items
  4. Identifiable patterns of answers
  5. Cheating and others (partly unclear)
  6. Vague instructions
  7. Speed of scoring

Reliability

Reliability is the consistency of measurement or stability of test scores over time or across different conditions. It is a fundamental psychometric property that determines the consistency, stability and dependability of test scores.

FormMeaning
1. Test-retestSame test given twice; scores compared
2. Pearson product moment correlationThe coefficient used to compare the two sets of scores (the notes write 'concurrent' beside it)
3. Parallel formTwo instruments that can be standardised
4. Internal consistencySplit-half (administer once, split items into even and odd halves; 0.7 and above is reliable), Cronbach Alpha, Kuder–Richardson
5. Inter-raterTwo or more experts rate the instrument

Cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains.

Meaning, Purposes and Process of Research

What research is, why it exists, its characteristics and its nine-step process.

Meaning of research

Research is the process of arriving at dependable solutions to problems through a planned and systematic collection, analysis and interpretation of data. It is the manner in which knotty problems are solved in an attempt to push back the frontiers of ignorance and so advance the frontiers of knowledge.

Research exists because a problem exists. Where there is no problem, or a solution has already been found, research is unnecessary or concluded.

Educational research is the application of the scientific method in finding solutions to educational problems.

Purposes of educational research

  1. Find solutions to school problems.
  2. Generate more knowledge.
  3. Provide data for decision making.
  4. Aid effective and efficient administration of educational institutions.
  5. Ensure the overall development of the educational system.
  6. Help in making new discoveries.

Characteristics of research

Research Design

The blueprint of a study and the ten types of design.

Meaning of research design

Research design is the overall blueprint, framework or master plan that guides a researcher in conducting a study. It specifies how data will be collected, measured, analysed and interpreted to answer research questions or test hypotheses accurately, systematically, economically and effectively.

Components in the definition:

  1. It is the plan or blueprint for the study.
  2. It guides the collection and analysis of data.
  3. It helps the researcher answer research questions or test hypotheses.
  4. It ensures the study is systematic and organised.
  5. It helps control errors and improve validity and reliability.
  6. It specifies procedures, methods and techniques.
  7. It saves time, cost and effort by giving clear direction.

Types of research design

DesignMeaning
1. DescriptiveDescribes characteristics, behaviours, opinions, attitudes or situations of a group, event or phenomenon without manipulating variables
2. ExperimentalDetermines cause and effect by manipulating an independent variable and observing the effect on a dependent variable
  (i) True experimentalUses randomisation
  (ii) Quasi-experimentalNo randomisation
  (iii) Pre-experimentalNo random assignment and usually no control group
3. SurveyCollects information from a sample through questionnaires, interviews or opinion polls
4. CorrelationalExamines the relationship between two or more variables without manipulating them
5. Ex-post factoStudies events that have already occurred to determine possible causes
6. Case studyIn-depth investigation of a single individual, group, institution, event or community
7. HistoricalStudies past events using documents, records and other sources to understand present and future trends
8. Cross-sectional(Listed in the notes without a definition)
9. Quantitative(Listed in the notes without a definition)
10. Qualitative(Listed in the notes without a definition)

Part I

Review of Related Literature

Theoretical and conceptual frameworks, conceptual review and how to review an empirical study.

A review of related literature (RRL) is a systematic, critical and organised examination of existing scholarly works relevant to a research problem. It is done under four subheadings:

  1. Theoretical framework
  2. Conceptual framework
  3. Conceptual review
  4. Empirical study (review)
SubheadingWhat it does
Theoretical frameworkDiscusses relevant theories that underpin the study: identify the applicable theory, explain how it relates to the research problem, justify adopting it
Conceptual frameworkA researcher's visual and narrative representation of the expected relationship among the study variables
Conceptual reviewExplains key concepts and variables: define major terms from authoritative sources, explain variables, clarify relationships among concepts
Empirical reviewReviews previous studies, especially findings related to the study: summarise past studies (author, year, method, findings), compare and contrast findings, identify consistencies and contradictions

Why we need a conceptual framework

  1. Explains the relationship among variables.
  2. Provides direction for the study.
  3. Guides hypothesis formulation.
  4. Guides data collection.
  5. Guides instrument development.
  6. Guides data analysis.
  7. Serves as a research assumption.
  8. Provides a basis for interpreting findings.

How to review an empirical study

  1. Authors and year of the study.
  2. Title or focus of the study.
  3. Purpose or objective.
  4. Research design (e.g. a correlational design was adopted).
  5. Population and sample: target population, sample size and sampling technique.
  6. Instruments for data collection (questionnaire, achievement test, rating scale).
  7. Method of data collection (e.g. a rating scale).
  8. Validity and reliability of the instrument (validated by experts; reliability coefficient obtained).
  9. Method of data analysis.
  10. Major findings: key results, whether hypotheses were accepted or rejected.
  11. Conclusion of the study.
  12. Relevance to the current study: link each study to your research.

The notes' example review

Example
Adebayo (2022) studied the relationship between test anxiety and academic performance among secondary school students, to find whether test anxiety significantly predicts achievement in mathematics. Design: correlational. Population: 2,500 students; sample: 300 by simple random sampling. Instruments: test anxiety scale and a mathematics achievement test, validated by experts, reliability 0.82 (Cronbach's alpha). Analysis: Pearson Product Moment Correlation. Finding: test anxiety had a significant negative influence on academic outcomes. Relevance: supports the influence of psychological variables on academic outcomes.

TypeMeaning

Qualitative data(The notes give no definition.)

Data gathering (collection) is the systematic process of obtaining information relevant to the research problem, to answer research questions or test hypotheses. It involves collecting facts, opinions, observations, measurements or responses from participants or other sources.

Data processing

Data processing is the organisation, preparation and transformation of raw data into a form suitable for analysis.

StageMeaning
1. EditingChecking collected data for errors, omissions, inconsistencies and accuracy
2. CodingUsing symbols, letters or numbers for easy classification and analysis
3. ClassificationGrouping data into categories based on similarities
4. TabulationArranging data into tables, rows and columns

Memory line, E C C T: Edit, Code, Classify, Tabulate.
